### What to notice first when smoke starts affecting your routine

Smoke exposure can make ordinary routines feel harder to judge. The useful move is to notice what is repeatable: where the exposure happens, how your body responds, and which small changes make the day easier.

### Look for the repeatable moment

If irritation shows up in the same place or at the same time, you have a starting point. A hallway, car ride, office entrance, or social setting each suggests a different practical adjustment.

### A simple visual check

Use the image here as a reminder to separate the exposure source, the setting, and the symptom pattern before deciding what to change.

### Choose one adjustment, then observe

For a week, test one change that is easy to repeat. Keep the change small enough that you can actually stick with it, then watch whether the pattern becomes clearer.

### Get help sorting the pattern

If the pattern is persistent or symptoms are interfering with daily life, a focused consultation can help you decide what deserves attention now.
