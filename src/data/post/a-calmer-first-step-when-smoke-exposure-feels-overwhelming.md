### A calmer first step when smoke exposure feels overwhelming

When smoke exposure becomes part of daily life, people often want one practical next step rather than a pile of advice. This short guide keeps the focus narrow: understand what changed, reduce avoidable exposure, and know when to ask for help.

### Start with the pattern, not panic

A useful first question is simple: when does the exposure happen most often? Morning commutes, shared housing, workplace drift, and weekend social settings call for different fixes. Naming the pattern makes the next decision less vague.

### Make one change you can repeat

Try one small adjustment for a week, such as changing the route you walk, improving ventilation before symptoms start, or keeping a short note of when irritation appears. The goal is not perfection. It is a clearer signal.

### Talk through your next step

If symptoms are persistent, worsening, or confusing, a short clinical conversation can help separate irritation from something that needs attention.
